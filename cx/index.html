What it is

The unit of account for century-scale obligations. Eight world assets at fixed weights, rebalanced every day: whoever owes the index owes CX, and the weights never change. A 25–80 year lease outlives every currency it could be written in — so the payment is a portfolio instead of a number, and the portfolio is the same one for everyone on every day.

Mechanics

  • Rent — a number of CX a year, for the life of the lease; measured in the index it never changes
  • Reset — annual, on the contract anniversary; the TWAP window ends 30 days before payment, so the tenant knows the invoice a month ahead
  • Settlement — the CX owed are converted at that day's fix into the payment currency, never capped — dollars, rupiah or satoshi are what the rent is paid in, not what it is
  • Nothing on top — no collar, no floor, no ruler: a clamp needs a unit outside the index, and that unit becomes a second bet over the basket
  • Oracle — a daily on-chain fix; on divergence the annex computation from named public fixes prevails — index disputes are arithmetic, never renegotiation

Pricer

What the index is for. The product is a leasehold — the right to use a parcel for a term, never its title. Part of its value is taken as a premium at signing, the rest returned as rent, indexed for the life of the contract. One thing moves: the indexation, so the lease can be stress-tested across what the index might do. The deal and the estate's underwriting are stated as the fixed figures they are, shown so the price can be checked rather than taken on faith.
